Diagnosis: DQN is not the problem — the benchmark has no headroom left

Your DQN is already optimal. I decoded the exact seed-42 PSL profiles the eval uses and compared every planned route against the true time-optimal path (time-weighted Dijkstra oracle):

- grid (N1, K=1): all 98/98 of DQN policy 5b6c4910's routes are exactly time-optimal. The oracle's mean is 169.7s vs Dijkstra's 208.7s — a maximum possible improvement of 18.7%. DQN's measured 15% is that ceiling minus ~11s/route of SUMO overhead (insertion, acceleration, junction crossing), which dilutes the measured ratio.
- rand_grid (N2, K=1): DQN 9a9355f4 is within 1.9% of the oracle (83/100 routes optimal). But the ceiling here is a shocking 3.8%, because of a bug-like design flaw: the net's lane speed limit is 13.9 m/s (50 km/h), and SUMO clips the vehicle to it. Your PSL "fast" edges (100 km/h = 27.8 m/s) physically run at 13.9 m/s, so the real contrast is 40-vs-50 km/h. The recorded eval times match the clipped model exactly, confirming this.

So no amount of model/reward/hyperparameter work can improve K=1 results — the agent has literally solved the benchmark. To your question "is this problem wrong for DQN": no — for K=1 it's a deterministic problem the DQN memorizes perfectly (which it did). The issue is the benchmark can't express a large win. Two secondary findings:

1. K>1 evaluation is open-loop. plan_route() in eval_trsp2.py freezes time_slot at leg start and plans the whole route; for T3 the slot_duration is 60s while a leg takes ~180s, so the plan is stale after one slot. The trained closed-loop behavior (agent sees the slot at every node) is never exercised at eval — this is why K=2–4 shows no extra benefit.
2. Of your six candidate levers, (5) traffic parameters and (6) network design are the real ones. Reward, state/action space, and the algorithm are fine (proof: it reaches the oracle). Hyperparameters only matter for scaling to a bigger network.

The plan

Phase 1 — Redesign the two benchmarks (this is where the win comes from)
- grid: regenerate as 7×7, edge length 600m, --default.speed 30 (current grid is already 30 m/s, so mainly resize). PSL: v_min: 15, v_max: 100, speed_step: 85 (keeps exactly two levels), p_slow = 0.3 → ~45% headroom. If you want a more conservative congestion story, {20,100} gives ~40%.
- rand_grid: regenerate (netgenerate --rand + your existing post_processing.py) with ~40–50 junctions, edge lengths 250–600m, and critically --default.speed 30 so PSL speeds are expressible. Then use the identical PSL band as grid — symmetric analysis across both networks. (The current 81–212m edges are also bad independently: per-hop overhead swamps traffic differences.)
- Add a speed_probs option to PSLTrafficModel._generate_random (weighted rng.choices) to get p_slow = 0.3. One small change; env and eval share the class.

Phase 2 — Scale DQN training to match (validated numbers)
- num_episodes: 12000–15000, epsilon_min: 0.01, hidden_dim: 128 (49 nodes → obs_dim ~99, 4× the OD pairs).
- Add a graph-only greedy probe eval during training: every ~250 episodes, plan a fixed set of OD pairs with the greedy policy on the graph (no SUMO needed) and log %-optimal and tt/oracle; save the best checkpoint, not the last. This also gives you clean convergence curves for the thesis, and %-routes-optimal is a much better reported metric than raw reward.
- Optional stabilizers (cheap, low-risk): Double-DQN target and Huber loss.

Phase 3 — Make K>1 evaluation closed-loop (this is where RL earns its keep scientifically)
- Change eval_trsp2.py to replan at each junction with the live slot (hop-wise execution like the training env, or traci.vehicle.setRoute at junction arrivals), instead of freezing the slot at leg start.
- Set slot_duration ≈ mean leg time (~300s) for K=2–4 so slots actually flip mid-leg. Static Dijkstra can't adapt; closed-loop DQN can — here the DQN-vs-Dijkstra gap can exceed the static headroom. Optionally add normalized time-within-slot to the observation so the agent can anticipate slot boundaries.

Phase 4 — Re-run and report
- Retrain the 8 (2 networks × K=1–4) DQN policies, re-run eval_trsp2.py, and report per-pair paired results vs Dijkstra plus %-optimal vs the computable oracle. Expected end state based on the validation: DQN ~40–45% faster than Dijkstra on both networks (vs 15% / 9% today), sitting within ~2% of the oracle.

Two cautions: don't push contrast beyond {10,100} — makespans balloon, episodes get long, and realism suffers; and keep the fixed seed-42 profile as the K=1 design (it's a "learned traffic-aware routing table" benchmark — legitimate, and your SVC track already covers generalization to live traffic). PPO/A2C's low success rates are a separate problem, untouched by this plan.
